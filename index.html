06 / ABOUT

About Me & Teaching Philosophy

How I create inclusive, curious, and resilient learning environments in the modern IT classroom.

I believe that learning technology is not merely about memorizing programming syntax or passing standardized tests—it is about cultivating a fearless mindset for problem solving. When students understand how computers think, they transform from passive consumers of digital products into active, creative builders.

In my classroom, mistakes are welcomed as the most valuable learning data. Debugging an error message is not a failure; it is an authentic puzzle that develops resilience and critical reasoning. Whether guiding a student through their first Python "Hello World" or helping an advanced class configure a routed virtual network, I focus on the "why" beneath the surface.

Beyond the classroom, I continuously experiment with new open-source educational software, design hands-on physical computing experiments with microcontrollers, and advocate for equitable computer science education for all learners regardless of their background.

Core Pedagogical Principles

01

Learn by Building, Not Memorizing

Students retain concepts best when applying them immediately to build games, websites, and working tools.

02

Debugging Fosters Resilience

Encountering errors is essential. Teaching structured troubleshooting turns frustration into triumph.

03

Demystify the Digital Box

Every piece of technology is made by people. By taking things apart, students realize they can build them too.

04

Inclusion & Equitable Access

Computer science is for everyone. Diverse classroom perspectives produce more thoughtful technology.
